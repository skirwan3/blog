## Background

According to [data from the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/opioids/data/index.html), more than
564,000 people died from overdoses involving any opioid — prescription or illicit —
between 1999 and 2020. Overdose deaths involving opioids have increased more than
eightfold since 1999. Opioids killed nearly 69,000 people in 2020 alone, and over 82% of
those deaths involved synthetic opioids. The CDC's
[NCHS Data Brief No. 428](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db428.htm) shows
the rate of overdose deaths involving synthetic opioids other than methadone (such as
fentanyl) rising 56% in a single year, from 11.4 per 100,000 in 2019 to 17.8 in 2020.

## Purpose

The opioid epidemic highlighted the need to reduce opioid administration in pain
management, but there was no established way to track a facility's performance toward
that goal. As a Healthcare Data Analyst at Brooks Rehabilitation Center for Data
Solutions, I led development of a visual analytics tool — built in SAS Enterprise Guide
and SAS Visual Analytics — that accurately tracks and alerts on opioid medication
administration at our inpatient rehabilitation facility. The tool was rolled out alongside
clinical processes aimed at reducing opioid administration, and it now validates
hospital-wide progress on that initiative.

## Methods

Medication data for 3M+ administrations were extracted from our EMR for inpatient
rehabilitation patients from January 2019 through March 2021. Several transformations
were required before analysis.

Many administered medications were **compound medications** — a mix of active drugs.
Since the project's focus was opioid administration specifically, the opioid component
dosage was extracted from each. For example, a Percocet 10/325 mg tablet contains 10 mg
of oxycodone (an opioid) and 325 mg of acetaminophen (not an opioid), so only the 10 mg
of oxycodone was recorded as a new field.

<CompoundMeds />

Some opioid medications are administered via a transdermal patch rather than a pill. For
that route, wear duration directly affects the dose received, so dose was calculated as
dosage rate × administration duration.

<PatchDose />

Because opioid medications vary widely in potency (1 mg of oxycodone is roughly 1.5×
stronger than 1 mg of hydrocodone, for example), all dosages were converted to **morphine
equivalents** — a standardized measure of opioid potency relative to morphine — using
conversion factors based on CDC guidelines.

<MorphineEquivalents />

To measure opioid reduction, each patient's morphine equivalents were summed over 24-hour
windows at admission, discharge, and weekly intervals (a "week 1" window, for instance,
began 7 days after admission). Percent change relative to admission was then calculated
for each timepoint. To focus on patients arriving on opioids from acute care, only
patients with an opioid administration in the first 24 hours were included.

## Results

In July 2021, the interactive SAS Visual Analytics tool went live, displaying historical
trends and changes in opioid administration for active patients.

<MeAdmissionDischargeChart />

<MePercentChangeChart />

The tool has since been adopted by our Multimodal Pain Group to evaluate progress on
opioid reduction goals, and it's now part of regular team meetings where physicians,
nursing and therapy staff discuss patient pain management and satisfaction.

To evaluate the tool's contribution to opioid reduction, we compared percent reduction in
morphine equivalents for the six months before and after implementation using a two-sample
t-test.

<Callout title="Result">
  Average percent reduction in morphine equivalents from admission to discharge rose
  from **25.3%** in the six months before implementation to **59.8%** in the six months
  after (p &lt; .0001).
</Callout>

<MeImplementationChart />

The tool also gives clinicians a patient-level view of daily morphine equivalents and
opioid administration, with more detail available when a specific day is selected.

## Discussion

This tool gives clinical teams a comprehensive view of the hospital's progress toward
reducing opioid administration. Its automated notifications provide physicians with
actionable, patient-level data to support clinical decision-making. As new pain
management interventions are developed, sound metrics are needed to evaluate their
impact on patient care — so beyond its clinical utility, this tool may also support
research applications as a measure of opioid administration and reduction.
